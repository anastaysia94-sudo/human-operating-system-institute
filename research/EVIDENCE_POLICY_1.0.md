# HOSI Evidence Policy 1.0

## Purpose
HOSI labels evidence so learners can distinguish strong findings from plausible ideas, lived experience, and unresolved questions.

## Source hierarchy
Prefer, in order appropriate to the question:
1. Current clinical guidelines and consensus statements from recognized authorities.
2. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses with sound methods.
3. High-quality randomized or controlled studies.
4. Strong observational studies.
5. Mechanistic/laboratory evidence.
6. Qualitative and lived-experience research for questions they are designed to answer.
7. Expert commentary and institutional interpretation.
8. Founder opinion or educational hypothesis.

## Confidence labels
- A — high confidence
- B — moderate confidence
- C — limited/mixed
- D — preliminary/emerging
- E — insufficient evidence

These are HOSI educational labels, not regulatory grades.

## Rules
- Do not fabricate citations or statistics.
- Do not infer causality from correlation alone.
- Do not generalize outside the studied population without explaining the limitation.
- Do not convert group-level neuroscience findings into individual diagnoses.
- Do not treat anecdotes as population-level proof.
- Record meaningful contradictory evidence.
- Record the date a medical claim was reviewed.
- For rapidly changing topics, use current authoritative sources.

## Treatment coverage
HOSI evaluates psychological, behavioral, interpersonal, educational, environmental, rehabilitative, and pharmacological approaches using the same evidence-first standard.

## Correction
Material errors receive a documented correction, revision history, and updated reference trail.
