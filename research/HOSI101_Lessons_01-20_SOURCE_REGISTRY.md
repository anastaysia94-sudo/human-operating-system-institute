# HOSI-101 Lessons 1–20 — Source Registry

**Scope:** Orientation + Neuroscience Foundations  
**Date:** 2026-09-16  
**State:** AI-assisted source discovery/metadata verification; qualified human full-text review pending  

This registry supports the first 20 canonical lesson manuscripts. Source verification is not publication approval, clinical review, or peer review by HOSI.

## Evidence-use rules

- Institutional philosophy is labeled as philosophy/governance rather than disguised as empirical fact.
- Educational neuroscience sources orient learners but do not justify individual diagnosis.
- Reviews and guidelines are appraised for directness; a recent publication date is not itself an evidence grade.
- Brain regions are taught within interacting networks rather than as one-region/one-function boxes.
- Neuroplasticity is taught as constrained biological adaptation, not unlimited “rewiring.”
- Developmental neuroscience uses group-level findings without turning age averages into predictions about one person.
- Brain-health material uses a life-course, multidimensional framework and avoids guaranteeing prevention or individual outcomes.

## Registered sources

### [F01] NINDS — Brain Basics
**Source:** National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke. *Brain Basics.*  
**Type:** Authoritative federal public-education resource  
**URL:** https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/public-education/brain-basics  
**Use:** Basic brain/nervous-system orientation; ordinary human-system baseline.  
**Limits:** Educational overview, not a systematic review or diagnostic tool.

### [F02] WHO — Brain Health
**Source:** World Health Organization. *Brain health.*  
**Type:** International public-health framework  
**URL:** https://www.who.int/health-topics/brain-health  
**Use:** Multidimensional, life-course definition of brain health and determinants.  
**Limits:** Population/public-health framework; not an individualized prevention guarantee.

### [F03] WHO — Optimizing brain health across the life course
**Source:** World Health Organization. *Optimizing brain health across the life course: WHO position paper.* 2022.  
**Type:** Normative position paper  
**URL:** https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240054561  
**Use:** Life-course determinants including physical health, environment, safety, learning/social connection and services.  
**Limits:** Broad public-health guidance rather than a claim-specific neuroscience experiment.

### [F04] National Academies — Reproducibility and Replicability in Science
**Source:** National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine. *Reproducibility and Replicability in Science.* 2019.  
**Type:** Consensus study report  
**URL:** https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/25303/reproducibility-and-replicability-in-science  
**Use:** Scientific-method literacy; reproducibility/replicability distinction; transparency; revision.  
**Limits:** Cross-disciplinary methods guidance, not a substitute for field-specific appraisal.

### [F05] Institute of Medicine / National Academies — Standards for systematic reviews

## Human publication gate

Before these sources are used to mark Lessons 1–20 reviewed/published:

- verify bibliographic metadata and exact versions;
- perform full-text review where claim wording depends on source details;
- record populations, designs, comparators, outcomes and limitations;
- search meaningful contradictory/superseding evidence;
- confirm claim-to-source directness;
- obtain applicable subject-matter, medical/safety, lived-experience, accessibility and assessment review;
- preserve corrections and review provenance.

**No source in this registry is itself an accreditation, diagnosis, treatment recommendation for a learner, or proof that a lesson has completed independent review.**
