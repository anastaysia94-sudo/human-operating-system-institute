# HOSI-101 Lessons 1–20 — Evidence / Overclaim Audit

**Audit date:** 2026-09-16  
**Scope:** Modules 01–02, Lessons 001–020  
**State:** AI-assisted claim/source and current-evidence audit; qualified human full-text appraisal pending  

This audit asks a narrower question than “is the lesson good?” It asks whether the draft language stays within the evidence and whether major scientific claims have an identifiable source family, limitation, and revision trigger.

A `PASS` in this document means only **no obvious AI-detectable overclaim remains after this pass**. It does not mean peer review, clinical review, accessibility approval, learner validation, or publication approval.

## Cross-cutting audit rules

Every lesson was checked for these failure modes:

1. institutional philosophy disguised as scientific fact;
2. diagnosis treated as identity;
3. one-region/one-function neuroscience;
4. correlation/connectivity treated as causation;
5. group findings converted into individual diagnosis/prognosis;
6. neuroplasticity converted into unlimited rewiring or recovery promises;
7. developmental averages converted into deterministic age rules;
8. population risk reduction converted into prevention guarantees;
9. lived experience converted into universal evidence;
10. educational content converted into treatment or medication instruction.

## Current-evidence re-check performed 2026-09-16

The existing source registry [F01]–[F25] was cross-checked against newer/current evidence where the topic was especially prone to oversimplification.

### Distributed cognition / cortical networks
A 2025 review asks how distributed cognition really is and concludes that cognitive variables are represented broadly but **not ubiquitously** across the brain. That supports HOSI’s “specialization + distributed interaction” framing while rejecting both strict cartoon localization and “everything everywhere.”

- PubMed PMID 41345295: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41345295/

### Cerebellar cognition
A 2026 review states that cognitive cerebellar involvement is now well established anatomically/clinically/neuroimaging-wise while the exact computational role remains unresolved. That supports Lesson 14’s split between established nonmotor involvement and unsettled unifying mechanism.

- PubMed PMID 41871032: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41871032/

### Human learning-related plasticity
2026 reviews continue to report measurable learning-associated structural/functional change while emphasizing heterogeneous methods, indirect biological interpretation, and inconsistent links between imaging change and behavioral improvement.

- PubMed PMID 41914785: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41914785/
- PubMed PMID 42105826: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42105826/
- PubMed PMID 42614766: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42614766/

### Adolescent development / variability
2025 longitudinal cortical-development work continues to support spatially heterogeneous age/puberty effects and substantial individual variability rather than a single finish-line model.

- PubMed PMID 40789655: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40789655/

### Brain health / cognitive-decline risk reduction
WHO’s 2026 second-edition guideline remains current and explicitly uses a life-course, risk-reduction framework with both recommendations and identified evidence gaps.

- WHO: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240123557

## High-sensitivity statements and required handling

### Diagnosis / identity
Lessons 1–4 and 9 do **not** claim diagnoses are useless, fake, or merely social labels. The course position is narrower: diagnoses can be useful maps for communication, research, access, and care while remaining incomplete descriptions of personhood.

### Neurological emergencies
Lessons 11, 14, 15, and 20 avoid symptom localization/triage exercises and state that acute concerning neurological symptoms require appropriate medical evaluation. Human medical-safety review remains mandatory.

### Brain imaging / biomarkers
Lessons 11–18 repeatedly state that group-level anatomy/activation/connectivity differences are not automatically individual diagnostic biomarkers. Any later curriculum claim of diagnostic performance must provide direct validation evidence.

### Neuroplasticity / rehabilitation
Lessons 6 and 17 prohibit unlimited-rewiring and effort-blame framing. They distinguish compensation, restitution, task-specific learning, transfer, and indirect imaging evidence.

### Development
Lesson 18 rejects a universal “finished at 25” rule, competence inference from brain maturation, and deterministic prediction of mental illness from developmental imaging.

### Brain health
Lesson 19 uses `risk reduction`, not guaranteed prevention. Modifiable risk is not moral responsibility, and health behaviors are situated within structural conditions.

## Contradictory / qualifying evidence status

No lesson required wholesale reversal during this pass. Important qualifications were explicitly retained:

- **regional specialization vs distribution:** both matter; cognition is broad but not literally ubiquitous;
- **limbic terminology:** historically useful, modern boundaries disputed;
- **cerebellar cognition:** supported, but exact general computation unresolved;
- **brainstem connectivity:** broad integration supported, causal direction remains method-limited;
- **connectivity:** structural, functional, and effective connectivity cannot be collapsed into one concept;
- **plasticity:** measurable changes exist, but mechanisms, timescale, durability, transfer, and functional meaning vary;
- **development:** group trajectories coexist with substantial individual variability;
- **brain health:** current guidance supports modifiable risk reduction while retaining uncertainty and equity considerations.

## Bibliographic / full-text queue

Before any reviewed/published status, a qualified human reviewer should:

- verify all registry bibliographic fields and exact source versions;
- inspect full text for claims whose wording depends on methods/results beyond abstracts/official summaries;
- record population, design, comparator, outcome, limitations, and directness;
- confirm that evidence ratings are appropriate claim-by-claim;
- search for meaningful contradictory/superseding evidence missed by this pass;
- document corrections and re-review when claim language changes.
