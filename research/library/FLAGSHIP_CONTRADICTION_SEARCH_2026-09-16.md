## Important boundary

This audit is not peer review, clinical review, publication approval, or individualized medical advice. A search finding can strengthen, narrow, complicate, or fail to materially change a bounded HOSI claim. `No material contradiction found` means only that this search did not identify evidence requiring reversal of the bounded claim; it does not mean the literature is complete or unanimous.

## CLM-0001 — distributed/interacting brain systems

**Bounded claim:** broad brain maps are useful, but cognition and behavior emerge from interacting distributed systems and region-level descriptions do not diagnose an individual.

### Current cross-check
- NINDS / NIH public brain-science material remains appropriate for broad anatomy/system orientation.
- 2025 review: *How distributed is the brain-wide network that is recruited for cognition?* (PubMed PMID 41345295) reviews localized versus broadly distributed accounts and concludes that cognitive variables are represented broadly but not ubiquitously across the brain.

### Result
**No material contradiction to the bounded claim.** Newer work reinforces the need to avoid both simplistic one-region explanations and the opposite claim that every cognitive variable is represented everywhere.

### Calibration
Keep evidence rating conservative for educational orientation because this card is not a claim-specific systematic review and should not be used for individual inference.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41345295/

## CLM-0002 — sleep homeostasis + circadian timing

**Bounded claim:** sleep timing/sleepiness reflect interacting homeostatic and circadian processes, with light as an important circadian cue.

### Current cross-check
- NHLBI continues to describe interacting sleep pressure and body-clock/circadian processes, with light/dark cues affecting the body clock.
- Borbély's 2022 review describes the two-process model as a major conceptual framework still relevant to sleep science.
- A 2025 alternative-perspective paper argues for refinements to how the two-process interaction is conceptualized rather than rejecting homeostatic/circadian interaction.

### Result
**No material contradiction to the bounded claim; meaningful model refinement exists.** HOSI should describe the two-process model as a useful framework rather than a complete literal mechanism.

Sources:
- https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep-deprivation/body-clock
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35502706/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41775810/

## CLM-0003 — CBT as an MDD treatment option

**Bounded claim:** CBT is an evidence-based psychotherapy option included in current guidance for adults with major depressive disorder.

### Current cross-check
- VA/DoD MDD guideline (2022) remains an active guideline family and includes effective psychotherapy resources.
- ACP's living MDD guideline recommends either CBT or a second-generation antidepressant as initial monotherapy for adults in the acute phase of moderate-to-severe MDD and suggests CBT monotherapy for mild MDD; treatment selection should incorporate benefits, harms, feasibility, comorbidity and patient preferences.
- ACP lists surveillance/update alerts through August 2025 for the living guideline.

### Result
**No material contradiction.** Current cross-guideline evidence strengthens the narrow statement that CBT is a guideline-supported option. It does **not** support universal superiority, guaranteed individual response, or treatment selection without patient context/preferences.

Sources:
- https://www.healthquality.va.gov/guidelines/MH/mdd/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36689752/
- https://www.acponline.org/clinical-information/clinical-guidelines-recommendations

## CLM-0004 — IPSRT evidence in bipolar disorder

**Bounded claim:** a randomized-study evidence base exists for IPSRT in bipolar disorder, but efficacy/comparative-effectiveness statements need full-text appraisal.

### Current cross-check
- VA/DoD bipolar guideline (2023) suggests psychotherapy as an adjunct to pharmacotherapy for non-acutely-manic Bipolar I/II, listing CBT, family/conjoint therapy, IPSRT and non-brief psychoeducation; it states evidence is insufficient to recommend one specific psychotherapy over the others.
- ISBD Psychological Interventions Taskforce position paper (2024) continues to treat psychological therapies as a meaningful part of bipolar care while highlighting research needs.
- 2025 review of international bipolar guidelines reports broad endorsement of psychosocial interventions while noting more research is needed on long-term outcomes.
- A small 2026 randomized IPSRT study reports symptom/rhythm benefits but explicitly calls for larger samples and longer follow-up.

### Result
**No material contradiction to the deliberately conservative claim.** Current evidence argues against upgrading this card to an unqualified statement that IPSRT is superior or universally effective. The strongest defensible current wording is that IPSRT is a recognized adjunctive psychotherapy with a developing evidence base, and comparative ranking remains uncertain.

Sources:
- https://www.healthquality.va.gov/guidelines/MH/bd/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38632696/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40411321/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41864720/

## CLM-0005 — bipolar care is multimodal

**Bounded claim:** current bipolar guidance includes psychological, recovery, monitoring, crisis and pharmacological care rather than treating medication as the only meaningful ingredient.

### Current cross-check
- NICE CG185 remains active with a 2025 update and broad multimodal scope.
- VA/DoD bipolar guideline (2023) includes diagnosis/triage, specialty care, mania/hypomania, bipolar depression, psychosocial/recovery-oriented therapy and collaborative/supportive care.
- A 2025 international-guideline review finds psychosocial interventions endorsed across major bipolar guidelines, generally as supportive/adjunctive treatment rather than medication replacement.

### Result
**No material contradiction.** The cross-guideline evidence strengthens multimodal treatment-literacy framing while also reinforcing HOSI's rule against blanket anti-medication claims.

Sources:
- https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg185
- https://www.healthquality.va.gov/guidelines/MH/bd/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40411321/

## CLM-0006 — ADHD diagnosis requires broader clinical assessment

**Bounded claim:** ADHD diagnosis should not be inferred from one rating scale, app score or isolated attention complaint; broader clinical assessment and multi-context information are required.

### Current cross-check
- Current NICE NG87 continues to require specialist/equivalent qualified assessment and explicitly states diagnosis should not be made solely from rating scales or observational data.
- 2024 systematic review of 231 ADHD diagnostic-tool studies found wide variability and generally low strength of evidence for individual tools; it concludes reliable diagnosis requires experienced clinician judgment supported by standardized ratings and multiple informants/settings.
- 2024 adult-rating-scale review supports rating scales as useful assessment tools, not standalone substitutes for clinical diagnosis.
- A 2025 systematic appraisal of ADHD clinical-practice guidelines confirms that multiple current guideline families remain relevant and vary in quality.

### Result
**No material contradiction.** Newer evidence directly supports the bounded anti-single-test claim. Rating scales remain useful; HOSI should not misstate that boundary as 'rating scales are useless.'

Sources:
- https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87/chapter/Recommendations
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38523599/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38369740/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40589651/

## CLM-0008 — current PTSD / acute-stress guideline literacy

**Bounded claim:** a current evidence-based guideline base exists for PTSD/acute-stress assessment and management; guidelines support treatment literacy but do not authorize Atlas AI or HOSI coursework to choose treatment for an individual.

### Current cross-check
- VA/DoD PTSD/Acute Stress guideline (2023; official page updated August 2025) contains 34 evidence-based recommendations across acute stress, assessment/diagnosis and management and explicitly does not replace clinical judgment.
- APA released a 2025 adult PTSD clinical practice guideline based on systematic evidence review, benefits/harms, patient values/preferences and applicability.
- APA explicitly states guidelines are central but not the only determinant of treatment choice; patient history/preferences and professional judgment matter.
- APA's 2025 treatment page recommends three first-line psychotherapies that fall within CBT-related approaches while also describing suggested medication options and the need for individualized professional discussion.

### Result
**No material contradiction to the broad guideline-literacy claim.** The 2025 APA guideline strengthens currency and cross-guideline coverage. It also reinforces the boundary that treatment guidelines do not equal individualized treatment selection.

Sources:
- https://www.healthquality.va.gov/guidelines/MH/ptsd/
- https://www.apa.org/ptsd-guideline
- https://www.apa.org/ptsd-guideline/treatments

## CLM-0009 — mild TBI/concussion cognitive symptoms and variable recovery

**Bounded claim:** mild TBI/concussion can include thinking, concentration and memory symptoms, and symptom patterns/recovery vary across individuals.

### Current cross-check
- CDC's 2025 mild-TBI/concussion symptom guidance states symptoms differ by person, can affect thinking/learning, mood, behavior and sleep, and most people improve over weeks while urgent danger signs require emergency evaluation.
- CDC's 2025 adult mTBI management resources reference updated evidence-based clinical policy work.
- VA/DoD's active post-acute mTBI guideline addresses assessment, recovery prognostication and symptom management for symptoms persisting beyond the acute period and explicitly does not replace clinical judgment.

### Result
**No material contradiction.** Current evidence strengthens the variability/prognosis boundary: general recovery patterns cannot be turned into a precise prognosis, clearance decision or rehabilitation plan for an individual learner.

Sources:
- https://www.cdc.gov/traumatic-brain-injury/signs-symptoms/index.html
- https://www.cdc.gov/traumatic-brain-injury/hcp/data-research/index.html
- https://www.healthquality.va.gov/guidelines/Rehab/mtbi/

# Cross-claim conclusions

1. **No initial claim required reversal** during this current-evidence search because the claims were deliberately narrow and uncertainty-aware.
2. Several claims should become **better sourced, not louder**:
   - CBT/MDD gains ACP living-guideline cross-checking.
   - Bipolar/IPSRT gains VA/DoD + ISBD/current guideline-review calibration.
   - ADHD gains a large 2024 diagnostic-tools systematic review.
   - PTSD gains APA 2025 cross-guideline evidence.
   - mTBI gains current CDC material plus the active VA/DoD post-acute guideline.
3. The search identified important **qualifiers rather than contradictions**:
   - IPSRT should not be ranked superior to other named bipolar psychotherapies from current VA/DoD evidence.
   - ADHD rating scales are useful aids, not standalone diagnostic verdicts.
   - PTSD guideline recommendations remain subordinate to individualized clinical context/preferences/judgment.
   - the sleep two-process model remains useful but is actively refined.
   - distributed-brain models still require nuance between localization and overgeneralized 'everywhere' accounts.
4. Evidence-card status may advance to `contradiction_searched` only after the structured record cites the relevant source IDs and preserves these limitations.
5. **Human full-text review remains mandatory** before curriculum/publication approval. Abstract/guideline-page verification is not a substitute.
