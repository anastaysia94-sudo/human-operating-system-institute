# HOSI-101 Lessons 21–37 — Evidence Hardening Audit

**Date:** 2026-09-13  
**Status:** AI-assisted evidence audit; independent human academic review still required.  
**Scope:** Lessons 21–37, with priority review of spacing, retrieval practice, misinformation, transfer, sleep, emotion, and stress.

This file does not mark any lesson published or clinically reviewed. Its purpose is to record where newer evidence supports, narrows, or complicates existing draft language.

## Audit conclusions

### Lesson 26 — Memory Distortion & Misinformation

**Current direction:** substantively sound, but one claim needs stronger sourcing.

The lesson correctly states that post-event misinformation can distort later memory reports and that individual susceptibility varies. A 2024 systematic review of nine adult studies found that several cognitive abilities were associated with reduced susceptibility, but no trait provided immunity. This supports the existing B-level language rather than any deterministic claim.

The lesson also says corrections can improve memory and beliefs but may weaken over time. That claim should be explicitly sourced in a later manuscript revision. A 2023 review on memory in misinformation corrections describes correction efficacy as dependent on recollection, repeated exposure, integration, source memory, and delay.

**Recommended revision:** keep the core claim; add the correction-memory review to References and label correction durability B rather than A.

### Lesson 31 — Spaced Practice

**Current direction:** principle is strong; applied generalization should be more cautious.

A meta-analysis of 29 studies on spaced retrieval practice supports a retention benefit when retrieval episodes are distributed over time. This reinforces the lesson's high-confidence description of the spacing principle.

However, 2024 evidence across nine introductory STEM courses found significant benefits in only some courses, with pooled conclusions sensitive to which courses were included. The authors concluded that generalizability across STEM courses remains unclear and may depend on task type, feedback, subject matter, and learner behavior.

**Recommended revision:** distinguish two claim levels:
- **A:** distributed practice generally improves delayed retention under many controlled conditions.
- **B:** the size and reliability of benefits in authentic classrooms depend on implementation and context.

The overall lesson evidence label should therefore be considered **A/B**, not an unqualified A, until a human reviewer confirms the applied-education wording.

### Lesson 32 — Retrieval Practice

**Current direction:** broadly supported and already appropriately calibrated.

The lesson distinguishes low-stakes retrieval practice from punitive/high-stakes testing and states that transfer is not automatic. This is consistent with the existing source base and with recent classroom literature showing implementation matters.

**Recommended revision:** no major scientific correction required in this pass. During full-text audit, verify the exact wording around feedback and productive difficulty against the cited educational reviews.

### Lesson 34 — Transfer

**Current direction:** appropriately skeptical and should remain so.

The lesson already distinguishes near from far transfer and rejects broad claims that training one narrow task reliably upgrades unrelated abilities. No newer evidence found in this pass justifies relaxing that stance.

**Recommended revision:** retain B/C calibration and require any future product or intervention claim to identify the actual trained task, transfer distance, comparator, and outcome.

### Lesson 35 — Sleep & Learning

**Current direction:** supported, but age- and memory-type variation deserve stronger emphasis.

A 2023 synthesis of 19 systematic reviews and meta-analyses concluded that sleep supports memory consolidation across the lifespan, particularly for complex declarative information, while effects vary by memory type and age. Procedural-memory findings in children were less robust, and sleep-dependent declarative consolidation appears reduced in older adults.

This strengthens the lesson's general claim while also supporting its uncertainty language.

**Recommended revision:** add the 2023 review-of-reviews; explicitly state that sleep-memory effects vary by memory domain and developmental stage. Keep the lesson at **A/B**, not a universal A.

### Lesson 36 — Emotion & Memory

**Current direction:** appropriately calibrated.

The existing draft correctly separates emotional salience and vividness from factual accuracy and does not claim that emotion uniformly improves memory.

**Recommended revision:** no major correction from this pass. Future review should prioritize systematic evidence on central versus peripheral detail and emotional-memory accuracy before any stronger claims are added.

### Lesson 37 — Stress & Memory

**Current direction:** appropriately calibrated.

The lesson already emphasizes timing-dependent effects of acute stress and rejects a simplistic cortisol story. The existing meta-analytic foundation remains suitable for the stated claims.

**Recommended revision:** no major correction from this pass. Future full-text audit should distinguish acute-stress findings from chronic-stress, trauma-history, and developmental claims rather than extrapolating across them.

## New evidence records

### A24 — Spaced retrieval meta-analysis
- **Citation:** Latimier A, Peyre H, Ramus F. A Meta-Analytic Review of the Benefit of Spacing out Retrieval Practice Episodes on Retention. Educational Psychology Review. 2020/2021;33:959–987.
- **Source type:** Meta-analysis
- **Included studies:** 29
- **Claim use:** spaced retrieval practice and retention
- **Evidence rating for HOSI claim:** A/B
- **Limitation:** pooled evidence does not imply an identical effect in every subject, classroom, or implementation.
- **Review status:** metadata/abstract checked; full-text human audit pending.

### A25 — Multi-course STEM spaced retrieval study
- **Citation:** Bego CR, Lyle KB, Ralston PAS, et al. Single-paper meta-analyses of the effects of spaced retrieval practice in nine introductory STEM courses: is the glass half full or half empty? International Journal of STEM Education. 2024;11.
- **Source type:** Multi-course applied educational study with single-paper meta-analysis
- **Claim use:** external validity and heterogeneity of classroom spacing effects
- **Evidence rating for HOSI claim:** B
- **Key limitation:** significant effects were not uniform across courses; pooled conclusions were sensitive to course inclusion.
- **Applicability:** useful as a counterweight to overgeneralizing laboratory spacing effects.
- **Review status:** metadata/abstract checked; full-text human audit pending.

### A26 — Misinformation susceptibility systematic review
- **Citation:** Brassil M, O'Mahony C, Greene CM. Do cognitive abilities reduce eyewitness susceptibility to the misinformation effect? Psychonomic Bulletin & Review. 2024;31:2410–2436.
- **Source type:** Systematic review
- **Included studies:** 9 adult studies; 23 reported associations
- **Claim use:** individual differences in misinformation susceptibility
- **Evidence rating for HOSI claim:** B
- **Key limitation:** evidence does not identify any trait that makes a person immune to misinformation.
- **Review status:** metadata/abstract checked; full-text human audit pending.

### A27 — Memory and misinformation correction review
- **Citation:** Kemp PL, Goldman AC, Wahlheim CN. On the role of memory in misinformation corrections: Repeated exposure, correction durability, and source credibility. Current Opinion in Psychology. 2023;56:101783.
- **Source type:** Review
- **Claim use:** durability and memory mechanisms of misinformation correction
- **Evidence rating for HOSI claim:** B
- **Key limitation:** correction efficacy depends on context and memory processes; this is not evidence that a single correction permanently erases misinformation.
- **Review status:** metadata checked; full-text human audit pending.

### A28 — Sleep and memory review-of-reviews
- **Citation:** Weighall A, Kellar I. Sleep and memory consolidation in healthy, neurotypical children, and adults: a summary of systematic reviews and meta-analyses. Emerging Topics in Life Sciences. 2023;7(5):513–524.
- **Source type:** Summary of systematic reviews and meta-analyses
- **Evidence base:** 19 systematic reviews/meta-analyses identified after full-text screening
- **Claim use:** sleep-dependent memory consolidation across age groups and memory domains
- **Evidence rating for HOSI claim:** A/B
- **Key limitation:** effects vary by memory type and age; some domains are less consistent than broad public messaging implies.
- **Review status:** metadata/abstract checked; full-text human audit pending.
