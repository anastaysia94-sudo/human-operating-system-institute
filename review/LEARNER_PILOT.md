# HOSI Learner Pilot Protocol

The learner pilot tests whether the public course experience is understandable, navigable, accessible and appropriately scoped. It is not a clinical study and should not silently become research.

## Preconditions

Before enrolling pilot learners:
- the tested content has an explicit version/ref;
- applicable subject-matter and safety gates are complete or the pilot scope is limited to non-clinical UX/content structure;
- accessibility issues known to block participation are resolved;
- privacy notice and consent language are available;
- no sensitive Atlas data is required;
- pilot participation is voluntary;
- participation is not conditioned on diagnosis disclosure;
- pilot data retention and deletion rules are defined.

## Minimum pilot tasks

Ask learners to:
1. find the course start;
2. identify the current publication/review status;
3. navigate to a specified lesson and back to its module;
4. explain the A–E evidence labels in their own words;
5. distinguish a diagnosis map from identity;
6. complete one formative self-check;
7. locate safety/scope language;
8. locate corrections/update information;
9. use a study-path navigation link without being asked to disclose a diagnosis;
10. locate the accessibility/help route.

## Measures

Collect only what is needed, such as:
- task completion success/failure;
- time-on-task where useful;
- navigation errors;
- comprehension errors;
- confusing terminology;
- accessibility barriers;
- misleading status assumptions;
- perceived cognitive load;
- free-text improvement notes.

Do not treat satisfaction as proof of scientific accuracy.

## Safety / ethics prompts

Explicitly ask whether any wording:
- felt diagnostic or identity-reducing;
- appeared to give personal medical advice;
- pressured disclosure of sensitive history;
- made a draft look final;
- made AI output look like the learner's own words;
- made it unclear how to correct/delete/export personal data.

## Accessibility participation

Pilot recruitment should include, where practical and ethical, people using different access needs and technologies. Do not require a participant to disclose a medical diagnosis to describe an accessibility barrier.

## Exit criteria

A learner pilot may support public-beta readiness only when:
- critical navigation/scope misunderstandings are corrected;
- no unresolved severe accessibility blocker remains in the tested scope;
- no repeated learner interpretation converts educational material into diagnosis/treatment instruction without corrective changes;
- all blocking pilot findings are resolved or explicitly accepted by the authorized human release decision-maker with rationale;
- a human pilot-review event is recorded.

Pilot success is not accreditation, clinical validation or proof of learning effectiveness across populations.
