**Scope:** Module 01 — Orientation; Module 02 — Neuroscience Foundations  
**Current state:** 20 individual source-verified draft manuscripts complete; AI-assisted evidence/overclaim audit complete; independent human review not yet performed.

## Purpose

This packet converts HOSI’s review gates into an executable process. AI output may prepare the material, but it may not pre-check a human-review box, invent reviewer identity, or mark a lesson reviewed/published.

## Reviewer roles

### 1. Subject-matter reviewer
Checks neuroscience, systems science, learning science, research-method, and developmental/public-health accuracy as applicable.

### 2. Clinical / medical-safety reviewer
Checks neurological emergency boundaries, diagnostic overreach, treatment/medication boundaries, developmental claims, brain-injury implications, and clinical applicability.

### 3. Lived-experience reviewer
Checks dignity, stigma, coercion, identity reduction, disclosure pressure, and whether heterogeneous experiences are represented responsibly.

### 4. Accessibility reviewer
Checks heading/navigation structure, alt text, cognitive load, readability, participation alternatives, screen-reader suitability, and assessment access.

### 5. Assessment reviewer
Checks alignment between objectives, quizzes, homework, Scholar challenges, instructor guidance, and scope.

## Module-level evidence gates

- [ ] Empirical claims are sourced; institutional philosophy is labeled as philosophy/governance.
- [ ] Claim wording matches population, method, outcome, and source limitations.
- [ ] Meaningful contradictory/superseding evidence has been searched for.
- [ ] Group-level neuroscience is not used as an individual diagnostic or prognostic test.
- [ ] Connectivity/correlation is not presented as causation.
- [ ] Mechanistic evidence is not presented as treatment efficacy.
- [ ] Brain-region teaching preserves both specialization and distributed interaction.
- [ ] Neuroplasticity language does not imply unlimited rewiring, guaranteed recovery, or effort-based blame.
- [ ] Developmental averages are not converted into fixed personal timelines or competence judgments.
- [ ] Brain-health risk reduction is not presented as guaranteed prevention.

## Safety / scope gates

- [ ] No lesson diagnoses a learner.
- [ ] No lesson directs medication or treatment changes.
- [ ] No exercise requires disclosure of diagnosis, trauma, neurological history, or treatment.
- [ ] Acute neurological-warning language appropriately defers to qualified/urgent medical care without attempting triage/localization.
- [ ] No brain scan, EEG, connectivity result, or classroom task is represented as a personal diagnostic test.
- [ ] Diagnosis remains a legitimate clinical/research construct and is not dismissed as merely identity or stigma.
- [ ] Lived experience remains distinct from population-level proof.

## Accessibility gates

- [ ] Every manuscript contains a meaningful original visual brief.
- [ ] Every visual brief has accessible alt text.
- [ ] Heading structure is predictable and logical.
- [ ] Technical terms are defined before assessment.
- [ ] Long exercises can be completed without personal disclosure.
- [ ] Rubrics grade reasoning rather than speed, diagnosis disclosure, or treatment beliefs.
- [ ] Review/module exercises do not create unnecessary working-memory or navigation barriers.

## Assessment gates

- [ ] Each quiz samples stated learning objectives.
- [ ] Homework evaluates learning rather than clinical status.
- [ ] Scholar challenges deepen method/evidence reasoning rather than merely adding difficulty.
- [ ] Instructor rubrics reward evidence calibration and uncertainty.
- [ ] Module reviews do not strengthen claims beyond the source lessons.

## Allowed reviewer decisions

- **APPROVE FOR NEXT GATE**
- **APPROVE WITH NON-BLOCKING CORRECTIONS**
- **REVISE AND RE-REVIEW**
- **HOLD**

`Published`, `Final`, and `Peer reviewed` are not reviewer decisions for a single gate.

## Correction record

For each requested correction record:

- lesson ID and section;
- original claim/issue;
- requested change;
- evidence/rationale;
- severity: blocking / non-blocking;
- reviewer and date;
- resolution commit;
- re-review status.

## PR-level exit criteria

A Lessons 1–20 PR may be considered for merge only when:

- [ ] all 20 lessons have applicable human review decisions;
- [ ] all blocking corrections are resolved and re-reviewed;
- [ ] claim/source audit is sufficient for merged claims;
- [ ] clinical/medical-safety review is complete where applicable;
- [ ] lived-experience review is complete where applicable;
- [ ] accessibility review is complete;
- [ ] assessment review is complete;
- [ ] deterministic curriculum QA passes on the final reviewed head;
- [ ] no false accreditation, publication, peer-review, deployment, or clinical-validation claim remains;
- [ ] a human with merge authority explicitly approves the merge.

A merge still does not make the entire 200-lesson HOSI-101 course complete, published, accredited, or clinically validated.
