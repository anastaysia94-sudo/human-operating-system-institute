## Standard architecture
Each lesson includes learning objectives, foundational human-system explanation, core lecture, evidence map, condition lenses, combination/comorbidity considerations, low-risk educational activity, Cornell Notes, knowledge checks, homework, scholar challenge, safety/scope statement, and references requiring verification.

## Core scholarly guardrails
- Complex behavior is not reduced to one neurotransmitter or one brain region.
- Mechanistic plausibility is not equivalent to clinical effectiveness.
- Group-level findings are not individual diagnostic tests.
- Lived experience is valuable contextual evidence but does not automatically establish population-level causality.
- Pharmacological, psychological, behavioral, interpersonal, educational, environmental, and rehabilitative approaches are evaluated by the same evidence-first standard.
- HOSI does not instruct learners to start, stop, or change treatment.

## Editorial note
The condition lenses are intentionally cautious. ADHD, bipolar disorder, OCD, PTSD/trauma, and TBI can involve overlapping systems, but overlap is not sameness and does not establish diagnosis. Evidence density will be explicitly reported in future research cards.

## Research direction
The final research layer should prioritize systematic reviews/meta-analyses for broad effects, randomized trials for intervention causality, longitudinal studies for trajectories, mechanistic studies for mechanisms, qualitative studies for lived experience, and current clinical guidelines for practice recommendations.
