## 2. Why this matters

Brain injury can affect attention, memory, thinking speed, emotion, sleep, and daily function, but outcomes vary widely. This lesson teaches a systems-first explanation so that learners can separate mechanism, context, and uncertainty from identity or blame.

## 3. Learning objectives

- Describe common cognitive effects after mild, moderate, or severe TBI.
- Explain why injury severity, location, recovery stage, and person matter.
- Distinguish compensatory memory strategies from claims of a universal cure or individualized rehabilitation plan.
- Explain why evidence-based rehabilitation still requires functional and contextual tailoring.

## 4. Vocabulary

traumatic brain injury; concussion; attention; memory; rehabilitation; compensatory strategy; external memory aid; prospective memory

## 5. Normal human-system baseline

Brain injury can affect attention, memory, thinking speed, emotion, sleep, and daily function, but outcomes vary widely.

## 6. Core science

[A] CDC guidance lists attention/concentration problems, slowed or foggy thinking, and short- or long-term memory problems among possible mild TBI/concussion symptoms. [S16]

[A] Moderate or severe TBI can also produce difficulties with learning, concentration, remembering information, communication, behavior, emotion, and independence; rehabilitation may help people relearn skills and improve function. [S17]

[A/B] “Memory after TBI” is not one deficit. Problems can arise from attention at encoding, slowed processing, executive dysfunction, fatigue, sleep disturbance, mood, or direct memory-system disruption. Recovery trajectories differ.

[A] INCOG 2.0 clinical-practice guidance for memory after moderate-to-severe TBI supports individualized internal and external compensatory strategies. It specifically recommends electronic reminder systems such as smartphone technology as a preferred external strategy for severe memory impairment, while emphasizing instruction that fits the person’s functional and contextual needs. [S26]

[B/C] Evidence should not be oversold. A 2024 systematic review of eight randomized controlled studies of compensatory cognitive training found heterogeneous protocols and inconsistent cognitive outcomes, describing the evidence as promising rather than definitive. This reinforces the need to distinguish a guideline-supported strategy class from a guarantee that any particular tool will help every person. [S27]

[A] New or worsening danger signs after head injury require medical evaluation; an educational platform must not substitute for emergency or rehabilitation services.

## 7. Historical context

Brain-injury care moved from broad lesion descriptions toward multidisciplinary acute care, neuropsychological assessment, rehabilitation, compensation strategies, and individualized return-to-activity planning. Modern guidelines increasingly emphasize functional relevance and contextualized strategy use rather than decontextualized drills alone. HOSI teaches the concepts, not an individual rehabilitation plan.

## 8. Evidence map

- A — Cognitive and memory symptoms are recognized after mild and more severe TBI. [S16][S17]
- A/B — INCOG 2.0 supports individualized internal/external compensatory memory strategies after moderate-to-severe TBI. [S26]
- B/C — Trial evidence for broad compensatory cognitive-training effects remains heterogeneous and inconsistent across protocols and outcomes. [S27]
- B — Exact cognitive profile and recovery course vary substantially among individuals.

HOSI ratings: **A = high confidence; B = moderate confidence; C = limited/mixed; D = preliminary/emerging; E = insufficient.** These are educational labels, not regulatory grades.

## 9. What is known

TBI can affect multiple cognitive systems, and severity labels alone do not fully predict an individual's day-to-day profile. Evidence-based memory rehabilitation can include compensatory strategies, but selection and teaching of those strategies should be tied to actual functional needs.

## 10. What is uncertain

HOSI cannot infer injury severity, prognosis, rehabilitation needs, or likely response to a specific cognitive strategy from self-report alone. Evidence also varies by injury severity, phase of recovery, targeted cognitive domain, intervention, and outcome measure.

## 11. Condition-specific applications

For persistent difficulties, qualified medical and rehabilitation professionals can assess function and tailor treatment. An educational recommendation to learn about external memory aids is not equivalent to prescribing a rehabilitation program.

## 12. Combination/comorbidity panel

TBI plus ADHD or PTSD can produce overlapping attention, sleep, arousal, and memory complaints. Historical timing, objective functional change, injury context, and alternative explanations matter; overlapping symptoms should not be assigned to one diagnosis by default.

## 13. Lived-experience perspective

People after brain injury may describe fatigue, slower thinking, losing the thread of a task, forgetting new information, or needing tools they did not need before. HOSI treats those reports as important functional evidence while avoiding assumptions about severity or prognosis.

## 14. Practical skill / exercise

For a fictional learner, compare several possible supports: smartphone reminders, written steps, one-task-at-a-time workflow, environmental simplification, and planned rest. Explain which functional problem each support is intended to address, what evidence level supports the strategy class, and why an actual rehabilitation plan would need individual assessment.

## 15. Safety / scope

This lesson is educational and not a diagnosis, return-to-play/return-to-work clearance, individualized rehabilitation plan, medication recommendation, or substitute for qualified medical care. New or worsening neurological danger signs after head injury require prompt professional evaluation; emergency symptoms require emergency care.

## 20. Homework

## 21. Instructor answer key / rubric

Require variability, overlap of mechanisms, rehabilitation scope, and medical escalation for danger signs. Full-credit answers recognize that guideline-supported compensatory strategies are individualized and functional, while systematic-review evidence still shows heterogeneity. Reject claims that one app, drill, supplement, or memory aid restores cognition universally.

Grade for conceptual accuracy, evidence calibration, reasoning, application, clarity, and scope/ethics. Do **not** grade a learner's personal beliefs, diagnosis, treatment choices, injury history, rehabilitation choices, or willingness to disclose lived experience.
