## 2. Why this matters

Brain injury can affect attention, memory, thinking speed, emotion, sleep, and daily function, but outcomes vary widely. This lesson teaches a systems-first explanation so that learners can separate mechanism, context, and uncertainty from identity or blame.

## 3. Learning objectives

- Describe common cognitive effects after mild, moderate, or severe TBI.
- Explain why injury severity, location, recovery stage, and person matter.
- Identify compensatory strategies without presenting them as individualized rehabilitation plans.

## 4. Vocabulary

traumatic brain injury; concussion; attention; memory; rehabilitation; compensatory strategy

## 5. Normal human-system baseline

Brain injury can affect attention, memory, thinking speed, emotion, sleep, and daily function, but outcomes vary widely.

## 6. Core science

[A] CDC guidance lists attention/concentration problems, slowed or foggy thinking, and short- or long-term memory problems among possible mild TBI/concussion symptoms. [S16]

[A] Moderate or severe TBI can also produce difficulties with learning, concentration, remembering information, communication, behavior, emotion, and independence; rehabilitation may help people relearn skills and improve function. [S17]

[A/B] “Memory after TBI” is not one deficit. Problems can arise from attention at encoding, slowed processing, executive dysfunction, fatigue, sleep disturbance, mood, or direct memory-system disruption. Recovery trajectories differ.

[A] New or worsening danger signs after head injury require medical evaluation; an educational platform must not substitute for emergency or rehabilitation services.

## 7. Historical context

Brain-injury care moved from broad lesion descriptions toward multidisciplinary acute care, neuropsychological assessment, rehabilitation, compensation strategies, and individualized return-to-activity planning. HOSI teaches the concepts, not an individual rehabilitation plan.

## 8. Evidence map

- A — Cognitive and memory symptoms are recognized after mild and more severe TBI. [S16][S17]
- B — Exact profile and recovery course vary substantially among individuals.

HOSI ratings: **A = high confidence; B = moderate confidence; C = limited/mixed; D = preliminary/emerging; E = insufficient.** These are educational labels, not regulatory grades.

## 9. What is known

TBI can affect multiple cognitive systems, and severity labels alone do not fully predict an individual's day-to-day profile.

## 10. What is uncertain

HOSI cannot infer injury severity, prognosis, or rehabilitation needs from self-report alone.

## 11. Condition-specific applications

For persistent difficulties, qualified medical and rehabilitation professionals can assess function and tailor treatment.

## 12. Combination/comorbidity panel

TBI plus ADHD or PTSD can produce overlapping attention, sleep, arousal, and memory complaints. Historical timing and objective functional change are important.

## 13. Lived-experience perspective

People after brain injury may describe fatigue, slower thinking, losing the thread of a task, forgetting new information, or needing tools they did not need before. HOSI treats those reports as important functional evidence while avoiding assumptions about severity or prognosis.

## 14. Practical skill / exercise

Redesign a fictional post-injury routine using external memory supports: alarms, written steps, one-task-at-a-time workflow, rest breaks, and environmental simplification.

## 15. Safety / scope

This lesson is educational and not a diagnosis, return-to-play/return-to-work clearance, individualized rehabilitation plan, medication recommendation, or substitute for qualified medical care. New or worsening neurological danger signs after head injury require prompt professional evaluation; emergency symptoms require emergency care.
