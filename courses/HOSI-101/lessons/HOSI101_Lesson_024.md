## 2. Why this matters

People can keep only a limited amount of task-relevant information active at once, especially when they must manipulate it or resist distraction. This lesson teaches a systems-first explanation so that learners can separate mechanism, context, and uncertainty from identity or blame.

## 3. Learning objectives

- Describe working memory as limited-capacity temporary maintenance/manipulation.
- Explain why working-memory limits matter for complex tasks.
- Evaluate claims that working-memory training broadly raises intelligence.

## 4. Vocabulary

working memory; maintenance; manipulation; central executive; phonological loop; visuospatial processing

## 5. Normal human-system baseline

People can keep only a limited amount of task-relevant information active at once, especially when they must manipulate it or resist distraction.

## 6. Core science

[A] Working memory is a family of processes supporting temporary maintenance and manipulation of information needed for reasoning, language, planning, and problem solving. Influential multicomponent models distinguish control processes from verbal and visuospatial storage functions. [S01]

[A] Capacity is limited, so long instructions, multitasking, and dense unfamiliar material can overload performance. External supports—written steps, checklists, diagrams, chunking, and reducing irrelevant demands—can compensate without assuming a deficit in intelligence.

[A/B] Training can improve performance on trained or closely related working-memory tasks, but high-quality meta-analytic evidence does not support a broad claim that computerized working-memory drills reliably increase general intelligence or far-transfer skills in healthy populations. [S14]

## 7. Historical context

The field moved from simple “storage box” metaphors toward interacting cognitive systems, computational models, neuropsychology, and network-level neuroscience. Historical models remain useful when treated as models rather than literal maps of a single person's brain.

## 8. Evidence map

- A — Working memory is limited and supports temporary maintenance/manipulation. [S01]
- A/B — Near-task training effects occur; broad far-transfer claims are not well supported. [S14]

HOSI ratings: **A = high confidence; B = moderate confidence; C = limited/mixed; D = preliminary/emerging; E = insufficient.** These are educational labels, not regulatory grades.

## 9. What is known

The highest-confidence statements are those labeled A above and tied to the listed source families.

## 10. What is uncertain

The best model of working-memory architecture remains debated; capacity depends on material, expertise, strategy, and measurement.

## 11. Condition-specific applications

ADHD and TBI can involve working-memory difficulties, but performance varies greatly and should not be inferred from a label alone. Stress may also impair working memory on average. [S08]

## 12. Combination/comorbidity panel

When brain injury, ADHD, sleep disturbance, or acute stress co-occur, use functional supports first and avoid attributing every error to one condition.

## 13. Lived-experience perspective

A person may describe the same cognitive phenomenon in different language: “blanking,” “brain fog,” “overload,” “I know it but can't get it out,” or “I never took it in.” HOSI treats these reports as valuable observations while keeping them distinct from diagnostic conclusions and population-level evidence.

## 14. Practical skill / exercise

Take a multi-step task you often lose track of. Move steps out of working memory into a visible checklist. Compare error rate and effort.

## 15. Safety / scope

This lesson is educational and not a diagnosis, treatment plan, medication recommendation, forensic memory assessment, or substitute for qualified medical/mental-health care. Do not use a single exercise or quiz score to infer a disorder. Urgent neurological or safety concerns require appropriate professional or emergency care.
