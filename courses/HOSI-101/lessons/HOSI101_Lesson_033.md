## 2. Why this matters

Learning new, complex material requires holding and connecting information while attention and working memory are limited. This lesson teaches a systems-first explanation so that learners can separate mechanism, context, and uncertainty from identity or blame.

## 3. Learning objectives

- Explain why limited working memory constrains learning.
- Identify avoidable extraneous load.
- Balance simplification with meaningful challenge.

## 4. Vocabulary

cognitive load; intrinsic complexity; extraneous load; worked example; chunking; expertise

## 5. Normal human-system baseline

Learning new, complex material requires holding and connecting information while attention and working memory are limited.

## 6. Core science

[A] Cognitive load theory builds on the limited capacity of working memory and argues that instruction should reduce unnecessary demands while preserving the demands essential to the task. [S21]

[A/B] Techniques such as worked examples, signaling, chunking, and sequencing can reduce avoidable load for novices. As expertise grows, supports may need to fade; what helps a novice can become redundant for an expert.

[B] The classic intrinsic/extraneous/germane terminology has evolved and is debated. HOSI uses cognitive load as an instructional design lens, not a complete theory of learning or a neurological diagnosis.

## 7. Historical context

The field moved from simple “storage box” metaphors toward interacting cognitive systems, computational models, neuropsychology, and network-level neuroscience. Historical models remain useful when treated as models rather than literal maps of a single person's brain.

## 8. Evidence map

- A — Working-memory limits are relevant to instructional design. [S01][S21]
- B — Specific load categories and measurement methods remain debated. [S21]

## 9. What is known

The highest-confidence statements are those labeled A above and tied to the listed source families.

## 10. What is uncertain

Cognitive load is not directly readable from a person’s brain or a single self-report rating.

## 11. Condition-specific applications

ADHD, TBI, sleep loss, and stress may increase functional difficulty under high load, but accommodations should be based on observed needs rather than stereotypes.

## 12. Combination/comorbidity panel

When multiple factors raise effort, prioritize removing irrelevant complexity before interpreting performance as lack of ability.

## 13. Lived-experience perspective

A person may describe the same cognitive phenomenon in different language: “blanking,” “brain fog,” “overload,” “I know it but can't get it out,” or “I never took it in.” HOSI treats these reports as valuable observations while keeping them distinct from diagnostic conclusions and population-level evidence.
