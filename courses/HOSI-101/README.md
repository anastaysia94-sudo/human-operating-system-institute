## Standard lesson architecture

Each canonical manuscript is expected to contain:

- publication/review metadata;
- original visual brief plus accessible alt text;
- learning objectives and vocabulary;
- normal human-system baseline;
- core science and historical context where relevant;
- evidence map;
- known vs uncertain claims;
- condition-specific and combination/comorbidity lenses;
- lived-experience perspective;
- practical skill/exercise;
- safety/scope boundary;
- Cornell Notes, reflection, discussion, quiz, homework, Scholar challenge;
- instructor answer guidance/rubric;
- references and further reading;
- research update log;
- “What would change our mind?” revision trigger.

## Publication gates

Before a lesson can be represented as reviewed/published, applicable gates include:

1. qualified claim-to-source/full-text review;
2. contradictory/superseding evidence search;
3. subject-matter review;
4. medical/safety review where applicable;
5. lived-experience review where applicable;
6. accessibility review;
7. assessment/rubric review;
8. blocking correction resolution and re-review;
9. final automated QA on the exact candidate;
10. explicit human approval.

## Integrity boundary

HOSI-101 is an educational course, not a diagnostic service, treatment directive, medical practice, or accredited university program. “Human operating system” is a teaching metaphor; people and brains are not literal computers.
